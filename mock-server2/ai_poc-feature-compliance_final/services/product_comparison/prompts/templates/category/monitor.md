Monitor-specific guidance:

## Audience Calibration

Monitor shoppers tend to research specs before purchasing and are comfortable with technical terminology. You may include specific spec values more freely than in other categories (e.g., "165 Hz refresh rate," "1ms GtG response time," "98% DCI-P3"), but always anchor them to a real-world scenario or use case. The spec can lead — but the "so what" must follow.

**Good:** "165 Hz refresh rate keeps fast-paced shooters and racing games looking fluid."
**Bad:** "165 Hz refresh rate with NVIDIA G-Sync Compatible support."

**Good:** "Covers 98% of DCI-P3, so colors in photos and video edits look close to how they'll appear in print or on screen."
**Bad:** "98% DCI-P3 color gamut with factory-calibrated Delta E < 2."

## Product Naming

Always refer to each product by its full model SKU (e.g., "27GP850-B," "32UN880-B," "34WP65C-B") — never by a shortened friendly name, marketing series name, or generic label like "Model A." Monitor buyers identify and search by SKU, so every quickPick, differentiator summary, and similarity should use the exact modelSku value from the product data.

## Key Dimensions to Evaluate

Prioritize differentiators from these areas when the data supports meaningful differences:

- **Picture Quality** — Panel type (IPS vs. VA vs. OLED), resolution, HDR tier, brightness, contrast ratio. Translate into viewing scenarios: dark-room gaming, bright-office readability, color-critical work.
- **Gaming Performance** — Refresh rate, response time (GtG), adaptive sync (G-Sync / FreeSync tier), Black Stabilizer or similar features. Frame in terms of game genres and competitive advantage.
- **Color Accuracy** — Color gamut coverage (sRGB, DCI-P3), factory calibration, hardware calibration support. Connect to workflows: photo editing, video grading, web design, everyday use.
- **Connectivity** — USB-C with Power Delivery (and wattage), DisplayPort version, HDMI version, built-in USB hub, KVM switch, daisy-chaining. Frame around desk setups: single-cable laptop docking, multi-device switching, peripheral management.
- **Screen Real Estate** — Size, aspect ratio (16:9, 21:9, 32:9), curvature, PBP/PIP modes. Translate into productivity scenarios: side-by-side documents, ultrawide multitasking, immersive gaming.
- **Design & Ergonomics** — Stand adjustability (tilt, swivel, height, pivot), VESA compatibility, bezel thickness, cable management. Frame around desk comfort and flexibility.

## Differentiator Labels

Use plain-English labels: Picture, Gaming, Color Accuracy, Connectivity, Screen Real Estate, Design, Ergonomics, Eye Comfort.

Avoid labels like: "Panel Technology," "Refresh Rate Specs," "I/O Ports," "Chromatic Fidelity."

## Similarities Guidance

For shared monitor features, explain benefits without just naming the standard:
- Adaptive sync → "Both reduce screen tearing and stuttering during gameplay without locking you to a fixed frame rate."
- IPS panel → "Wide viewing angles so the picture stays consistent whether you're centered or off to the side."
- HDR support → "Brighter highlights and deeper contrast in HDR-supported games and video content."
- Flicker-free / low blue light → "Built-in eye comfort features for long work or gaming sessions."
- VESA mount → "Both mount to a standard desk arm or wall bracket if you prefer to free up desk space."

## Constructive Framing for Lower Specs

- Lower refresh rate → "Smooth enough for everyday use, productivity, and casual gaming."
- Smaller gamut → "Accurate sRGB coverage handles web content, office work, and general photo viewing well."
- Fewer ports → "Straightforward connectivity — plug in and go without extra configuration."
